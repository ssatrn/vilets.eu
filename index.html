why i like plain websites

oct 13, 2026 · 3 min read

i keep coming back to plain websites. a page that loads instantly, reads well on any screen and does one thing is rare now, and i notice it every time i find one.

most of what makes a site feel heavy is stuff nobody asked for: popups, trackers, scripts that fight each other. take those away and what's left is words and a few links, which is usually all i wanted.

so this site is deliberately small. one file, one font, a couple of colors. if i want to change something i can open it and see the whole thing at once.

keeping a tidy folder

sep 28, 2026 · 2 min read

i used to spend more time looking for files than using them. the fix was boring: fewer folders, clearer names, and a five minute sweep once a week.

i name things with the date first, then a short description. sorting does the rest. anything i haven't touched in a year goes into an archive folder instead of getting deleted, so nothing feels risky.

it isn't exciting, but it takes a small, constant annoyance out of the week.

small tools i keep using

sep 2, 2026 · 3 min read

a short list, mostly for future me. a plain text editor that opens instantly. a notes file i never organize. a timer for focused work. and a paper notebook for anything i need to think through slowly.

none of these are clever. what they share is that they stay out of the way, and i've never had to learn a new feature just to keep using them.

i try new tools all the time, but only the ones that survive a few months get a spot here.

winter mornings

dec 19, 2025 · 2 min read

the best part of winter is the first hour of the day. it's dark, it's quiet, and the air is sharp enough to wake you up before the coffee does.

i started taking a short walk before opening my laptop. twenty minutes, same route, no phone. it changed how the rest of the day goes more than any productivity trick i've tried.

cold weather makes everything feel a bit more deliberate, and i like that.

learning quietly

jun 4, 2025 · 3 min read

for a long time i thought i had to announce what i was learning for it to count. it turns out most of the useful learning happens before anyone is watching.

now i keep a private log of what i tried, what broke, and what finally worked. when something is worth sharing, it's already half written.

the log is also kind to me on bad weeks. i can scroll back and see that things moved, even when it didn't feel like it.
